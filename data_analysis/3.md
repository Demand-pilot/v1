## 1. Deep-Dive Executive Overview & Core Business Problem

### A. The Core Supply Chain Dilemma
Every major supermarket enterprise operates on a tight economic wire. If a store runs out of essential items—such as milk, rice, or personal care products—customers walk out empty-handed, resulting in **immediate revenue loss and damaged brand loyalty**. Conversely, if a store orders too much inventory, millions of dollars in working capital are trapped on backroom pallets, risking **product expiration, warehouse congestion, and holding cost inflation**.

### B. Why Traditional Single-Model Forecasting Fails
Traditional retail planning systems attempt to apply a **single, uniform algorithm** (e.g., standard ARIMA or a single global neural network) across the entire enterprise. This single-model approach collapses in complex retail operations because retail demand patterns are fundamentally heterogeneous:
1. **High-Volume Staples**: Categories like *Grocery I* and *Beverages* sell thousands of units daily with smooth, continuous momentum.
2. **Promo-Elastic Surge Items**: Categories like *School & Office Supplies* generate modest baseline sales for most of the year, but surge by over **+145%** during back-to-school promotional seasons.
3. **Permanent Zero Series**: Out of 1,782 store-category pairs, **53 series generate exactly zero sales** for years (e.g., *Books* in 28 stores). Forcing an ML model onto zero series causes artificial stock orders.

### C. The DemandPilot Value Proposition
**DemandPilot** solves this fundamental business problem through an **Adaptive Multi-Model Mediation Architecture**. Instead of forcing one model onto all products, DemandPilot automatically profiles every store-category series, runs parallel prediction candidates (PyTorch Deep Learning LSTM for smooth staples vs. LightGBM Gradient Boosted Trees for promotional surges vs. Hardcoded Zero Masks), evaluates historical accuracy through rolling 16-day backtests, and routes each item to its optimal engine.

## 2. Understanding the Data Architecture: 4.5 Years History vs. 16-Day Forecast Horizon

### A. What is the 4.5-Year Historical Training Period?
To train machine learning models to anticipate annual holidays, regional school calendars, and macroeconomic shocks, models require extensive historical depth. The DemandPilot dataset spans **4.5 years of continuous daily transaction records** from **January 1, 2013 to August 15, 2017** ($1,684$ days of continuous sales history per series, totaling **3,000,888 individual records**).

This multi-year historical dataset allows DemandPilot to learn:
* **Annual Seasonality**: December Christmas shopping spikes, New Year closures, and annual back-to-school seasons.
* **Bi-Weekly Cash-Flow Cycles**: The recurring sales surge on the 16th and 1st of every month following payday disbursements.
* **Macroeconomic Shock Recovery**: How consumer purchasing shifted post-April 2016 magnitude 7.8 earthquake.

### B. What is the "16-Day Forecast Horizon"?
In retail supply chain operations, inventory replenishment is planned in bi-weekly operational blocks. The **16-day forecast horizon** represents the exact future window (**August 16, 2017 to August 31, 2017**) for which daily unit sales must be predicted. 

**Why 16 Days?**
* **Supplier Lead Time Alignment**: Most enterprise suppliers require 7 to 14 days of advance notice to process, pack, and ship bulk purchase orders to regional warehouses.
* **Store Inventory Scheduling**: A 16-day window allows store managers to align shelf restocking schedules with expected payday demand spikes (such as August 31st payday).

### C. How the 1,782 Parallel Series are Calculated
A time series is a continuous sequence of daily sales numbers over time for one specific item at one specific location. In DemandPilot:
$$\text{Total Parallel Time Series} = 54 \text{ Store Locations} \times 33 \text{ Product Families} = 1,782 \text{ Parallel Series}$$

Each of the $1,782$ series represents a unique operational unit (e.g., `Store 14` $\times$ `School Supplies` daily sales from 2013 to 2017).

### A. Regional Climate & Academic Calendar Split (Sierra vs. Costa)
Ecuador is geographically divided into two major economic zones with different school calendars:
1. **Sierra Region (Highlands, e.g., Quito, Cuenca)**: The academic year begins in **late August**. School supplies demand surges violently during August 16–31 (our exact 16-day forecast horizon!).
2. **Costa Region (Coastal Lowlands, e.g., Guayaquil, Manta)**: The academic year begins in **April**. School supplies demand in coastal stores peaks in April and remains quiet in August.

*Strategic Value*: DemandPilot's spatial store profiling prevents applying Sierra school season demand multipliers to coastal stores.

### B. Store Types and Operational Clusters
The 54 stores are categorized into 5 distinct store types (`Type A, B, C, D, E`) and 17 operational clusters based on store size and product assortment:
* **Type A (Large Hypermarkets)**: High volume across all 33 product families, including hardware and appliances.
* **Type D & E (Neighborhood Supermarkets)**: Compact stores focused almost exclusively on Grocery, Beverages, and Fresh Produce.

### C. Store Lifespans & Pre-Opening Zeroes
Not all 54 stores operated for the entire 4.5-year historical period:
* **Store 52** opened on April 20, 2017—meaning it has **1,570 pre-opening zero days** (over 4 years of non-existence).
* If a model trained on full 4.5-year timelines without store lifespan awareness, it would misinterpret 4 years of non-existence as zero consumer demand. DemandPilot automatically truncates history to begin on each store's official grand opening date.

### D. Local City Holidays vs. National Holidays
* National holidays (e.g., Christmas, New Year) affect all 54 stores simultaneously.
* Local city holidays (e.g., *Guayaquil Foundation Day* on July 25 or *Fundación de Quito* on Dec 6) impact only stores within that specific city. Sales drop by -6.91% on local holiday days in celebrating cities while neighboring cities operate normally.

## 4. Key Empirical Insights from Data Profiling

### A. The 2016 Earthquake Panic Surge (+20.2% Lift)
On April 16, 2016, a magnitude 7.8 earthquake struck Ecuador. Panic buying caused an immediate **+20.23% overall sales surge**, driven by essential survival items:
* **Personal Care**: +41.7% increase
* **Grocery I**: +39.6% increase
* **Home Care**: +31.6% increase
* **Beverages**: +26.7% increase

*DemandPilot Handling*: Encodes a 30-day emergency shock decay feature to prevent ML models from misinterpreting a one-time natural disaster as an annual recurring April surge.

### B. The Bi-Weekly Payday Peak (+8.0% Sales Jump)
Salaries in Ecuador are disbursed on the 15th and last day of every month. Consumer spending surges on the **day AFTER payday (16th and 1st of the month)** with an average **+8.0% sales lift**. Mid-week paydays boost sales significantly (+7.5% on Wednesdays), whereas weekend paydays show 0% lift because bank transfers process on Monday morning.

## 6. Human-Readable Inventory Replenishment Formulas

DemandPilot converts daily sales predictions into clear inventory replenishment rules:

### 1. Reorder Point (ROP)
Triggers a new supplier purchase order when store inventory drops below expected lead-time demand plus safety buffer:
> **Reorder Point = (Average Daily Forecast × Supplier Lead Time in Days) + Safety Stock**

### 2. Safety Stock (SS)
Protects store shelves against demand surges and supplier delivery delays:
> **Safety Stock = Service Level Factor × Demand Variability × Square Root of Lead Time**

*Example*: For 95% service confidence during payday surges, Safety Stock ensures store shelves remain fully stocked even if supplier trucks encounter highway delays.
