# DemandPilot — Master System Architecture & Data Analysis Synthesis Report

**Track**: AI for Business  
**Project**: DemandPilot — Adaptive Retail Demand Intelligence Engine  
**Dataset Scope**: 54 Supermarket Stores × 33 Product Families (1,782 Parallel Demand Series) over a 16-Day Forecast Horizon (August 16 – August 31)

---

## 1. Executive Summary for Leadership & Judges

Every retail enterprise faces a continuous balancing act: **having enough inventory on store shelves to meet customer demand without tying up millions of dollars in excess overstock**. 

Traditional forecasting systems fail because they try to force a **single fixed algorithm** across every item in the store. In reality:
* High-volume staples like **Grocery and Beverages** sell steadily every single day.
* Seasonal items like **School & Office Supplies** sit quietly for months and then suddenly surge by over 145% during back-to-school season.
* 53 specialty series (like **Books** in 28 stores) generate exactly **zero sales** for years.

**DemandPilot** solves this by deploying an **Adaptive Multi-Model Engine**. Instead of guessing, DemandPilot automatically profiles every product family, runs parallel predictions (Deep Learning LSTM vs. Gradient Boosted Trees vs. Automated Rules), tests their historical accuracy, and routes each item to the best-performing model.

## 2. Key Insights from the Data Analysis

Our deep analysis of over 3 million retail transaction records uncovered key patterns that directly shape how DemandPilot makes predictions:

### A. The 2016 Earthquake Panic Surge (+20.2% Lift)
* **What Happened**: On April 16, 2016, a magnitude 7.8 earthquake struck Ecuador.
* **The Impact**: Immediate panic buying caused a **+20.23% overall sales surge**. Personal Care items jumped by **+41.7%**, Grocery staples by **+39.6%**, and Home Care by **+31.6%**.
* **How DemandPilot Handles It**: Adds an explicit 30-day emergency shock decay indicator so our models don't confuse a one-time crisis with a permanent annual sales trend.

### B. The Bi-Weekly Payday Peak (+8.0% Sales Jump)
* **What Happened**: Salaries in Ecuador are paid on the 15th and last day of every month.
* **The Impact**: Stores experience an **+8.0% sales surge on the day AFTER payday** (the 16th and the 1st of the month). Mid-week paydays boost sales significantly (+7.5% on Wednesdays), whereas weekend paydays show no extra lift.
* **How DemandPilot Handles It**: Creates dedicated "day-after-payday" calendar flags and weekday interaction rules to ensure stock is ready on payday mornings.

### C. Store Lifespans & Delayed Openings
* **What Happened**: Not all 54 stores opened at the same time. For example, Store 52 opened 1,570 days after the dataset began.
* **The Impact**: Including thousands of artificial "zero" days before a store opened corrupts rolling averages and confuses machine learning models.
* **How DemandPilot Handles It**: Automatically truncates timeline history to begin on each store's actual grand opening date.

### D. The Oil Price Myth
* **What Happened**: Ecuador is an oil-dependent economy. At first glance, daily sales and crude oil prices show a strong negative correlation (-0.6280).
* **The Reality**: This was a coincidental macro trend—retail sales naturally grew over 5 years while global oil prices crashed. Within any single year, the real correlation is almost zero (0.0011).
* **How DemandPilot Handles It**: Uses "Delta Oil" (day-to-day oil price change) instead of raw oil prices, isolating real economic shocks while removing misleading long-term trends.

## 4. Human-Readable Inventory Replenishment Formulas

DemandPilot converts raw daily sales forecasts into clear, actionable inventory rules for store managers and warehouse planners:

### 1. Reorder Point (ROP)
Determines the exact stock level that triggers a new supplier order:
> **Reorder Point = (Average Daily Forecast × Supplier Lead Time in Days) + Safety Stock**

### 2. Safety Stock (SS)
Provides a protective inventory buffer against unexpected surge demand:
> **Safety Stock = Service Confidence Factor × Demand Variability × Square Root of Lead Time**

*Example*: For high-confidence 95% service levels during peak payday rushes, Safety Stock ensures store shelves never run dry even if delivery trucks are delayed.
