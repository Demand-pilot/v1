## 1. Problem Understanding

### Executive Summary
Small retailers and e-grocery businesses (including quick-commerce platforms like Zepto, Instamart, and local supermarkets) operate under a challenging business model characterized by thin product profit margins and high operational, warehousing, and fulfillment costs[cite: 2, 3]. In this environment, inventory miscalculation directly undermines profitability[cite: 2, 3].

Retailers face a continuous, costly dilemma[cite: 2]:
* **Overstocking:** Leads to excessive holding costs, capital lockup, and high food spoilage or waste on items with short storage cycles[cite: 2, 3].
* **Understocking:** Causes immediate stock-outs, uncaptured sales revenue, and permanent customer churn[cite: 2].

### Core Pitfalls of Existing Approaches
1. **Reliance on Guesswork & Moving Averages:** Many small and medium retailers rely on static historical averages or manual intuition, failing to capture subtle seasonality, daily sales trends, and promotional demand lifts[cite: 2].
2. **The "Single-Model" Fallacy:** Existing automated platforms often apply a single machine learning model across all store inventory[cite: 2]. However, empirical retail AI research (Gołąbek et al.) proves that no single algorithm excels across all product categories—Deep Learning (LSTMs) dominates staple foods, whereas decision trees outperform neural networks on highly price-elastic items like beverages[cite: 2, 3].
3. **Qualitative Operational Blindness:** Traditional forecasting algorithms operate solely on historical quantitative sales data[cite: 2, 3]. They are blind to real-world operational disruptions such as supplier bottlenecks, temporary store closures, or local road events, causing major forecast errors[cite: 2].
4. **The "Prediction-to-Action" Gap:** Data outputs from standard ML models (such as raw numeric arrays) do not provide non-technical store managers with clear, operational stocking decisions[cite: 2].

## 2. Target Users

Demand Pilot is built for key stakeholders across the retail and e-grocery supply chain:

### Persona 1: Dark Store & Micro-Warehouse Managers (e.g., Zepto, Instamart, Blinkit)
* **Context & Operational Needs:** Manage fast-moving SKUs with high turnover and ultra-short delivery SLAs[cite: 2, 3]. They are constrained by limited dark store footprint and refrigeration space, requiring item-level daily order quantities to prevent stock-outs without overfilling physical space[cite: 2].
* **Key Value Delivered:** Automated daily reorder quantities, early stock-out risk indicators, and operational override controls via the Store Knowledge Layer[cite: 2].

### Persona 2: Independent Supermarket & E-Grocery Owners
* **Context & Operational Needs:** Suffer from high holding costs and food wastage on perishable inventory[cite: 2]. They lack dedicated in-house data science or analytics teams and require simple, visual tools to replace guesswork[cite: 2].
* **Key Value Delivered:** Intuitive visual dashboard (Next.js/Three.js) combined with plain-English order rationales generated by an open-weight LLM agent[cite: 2].

### Persona 3: Supply Chain & Category Managers
* **Context & Operational Needs:** Oversee inventory health across multiple stores and product families[cite: 2]. They need visibility into model performance, demand volatility, and regional stock risk[cite: 2].
* **Key Value Delivered:** Aggregate stock health overview and adaptive model routing transparency (LSTM vs. LightGBM performance)[cite: 2].
